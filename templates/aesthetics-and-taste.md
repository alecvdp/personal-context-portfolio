# Aesthetics and Taste

## What This File Is For

What you're drawn to — across everything. Music, spaces, design, language, media. Taste is one of the most revealing and least documented aspects of a person. It tells an AI what "good" means to you without you having to define it every time. It's also one of the files that makes working with an AI actually feel personal rather than generic.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user articulate their taste — what they're drawn to and what repels them, across all the domains that matter to them. Taste is hard to articulate because it's felt more than thought. Help them by asking for specifics and examples. "I like minimalism" means nothing. "I like when there's one really good thing in an empty space" means something. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. What are you drawn to? Across anything — music, design, spaces, writing, food. What catches you?
2. Is there a particular aesthetic or vibe you keep returning to — something you'd recognize across different parts of your life?
3. What repels you? What makes you immediately want to leave a room, close a tab, turn something off?
4. What kind of environment do you do your best thinking in? What about that space works for you?
5. If someone was building something for you — a room, a playlist, a tool — and they wanted it to feel like you, what would it need?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. This file should be vivid and specific. If it could describe anyone, it's not done.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask if reading it back feels like looking in a mirror or reading a horoscope. Push for specificity if it's too generic.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Aesthetics and Taste

## What I'm Drawn To

[What catches them — across any domain. Specific examples and patterns. Not just "I like jazz" but what about it, what specifically.]

## The Thread

[If there's a common aesthetic that runs through their tastes — a vibe, a quality, a sensibility that connects seemingly different preferences.]

## What Repels Me

[What they actively dislike or avoid. Aesthetic dealbreakers. Environments, sounds, styles, patterns that push them away.]

## Where I Think Best

[The physical or environmental conditions that support their best work and thinking. What that space looks, sounds, and feels like.]

## What "Mine" Feels Like

[The sensory fingerprint — if you were building something that felt like this person, what would it need? The closest thing to a design brief for their inner world.]
```
