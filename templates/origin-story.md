# Origin Story

## What This File Is For

Where you come from — not a biography or a timeline, but the story you tell yourself about how you got here. The turning points, the through-line, the things that shaped you. This is the file that gives any AI working with you a sense of the person behind the context.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user create their origin story file — a narrative context document that captures where they come from and how they got here. This is not a resume or a timeline. You're looking for the story — the moments that mattered, the shifts, what connects them. Ask the following questions one at a time. Follow up when something interesting surfaces. When you have enough to draft, stop asking and draft the file using the output structure below.

**Questions to ask:**

1. Where did you grow up? What was the world like around you?
2. When you think about what shaped you — the things that made you who you are — what comes to mind?
3. Was there a moment or a period where things shifted — where you became a different version of yourself?
4. How did you end up where you are now? Not the career path — the actual story of how one thing led to another.
5. When you look back at all of it, is there a through-line you see? Something that connects the different chapters?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. This file should read like a person telling their story at a quiet dinner — not a memoir, not a timeline. Keep it to one strong page.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask the user to identify anything that doesn't sound right or feels off. This is their story — the words need to feel like theirs.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Origin Story

## Where I Come From

[The early context — place, environment, family, whatever shaped the starting point. Not a full childhood history — the parts that matter.]

## What Shaped Me

[The forces, events, people, or conditions that made them who they are. Specific, not generic.]

## Turning Points

[The moments where things shifted. Could be one, could be several. The inflection points in the story.]

## The Through-Line

[If they see a pattern connecting the chapters of their life — what is it? If they don't see one, that's worth noting too.]
```
