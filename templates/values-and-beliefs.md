# Values and Beliefs

## What This File Is For

What you actually orient around — not the values you'd put on a poster, but the ones visible in your choices. This file helps any AI working with you understand what matters to you, what you'd defend, and where your lines are. The best version of this file is specific enough that someone could predict what you'd do in a situation they've never seen you in.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user identify their actual values and beliefs — the ones that show up in how they live, not just the ones they'd claim. Push past abstractions. "I value honesty" is a starting point, not an answer. "I'd rather make someone uncomfortable with the truth than let them operate on bad information" is what you're looking for. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. If I watched how you spend your time and make decisions for a month, what values would I see showing up — even if you never named them?
2. Is there a belief or principle that guides you that most people in your world wouldn't share? Something that makes you a little unusual?
3. What's something you used to believe strongly that you've changed your mind about? What changed it?
4. What would you refuse to compromise on, even if it cost you something real?
5. Is there a value you aspire to but know you don't consistently live? Something you're working toward?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. The best values files are specific and honest. Avoid anything that could apply to anyone.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask the user to identify anything that feels aspirational rather than true. This file needs to describe how they actually are, not how they wish they were.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Values and Beliefs

## Core Values

[The values visible in their actual behavior — specific, honest, not generic. Each one should be a sentence or two that captures how it actually shows up.]

## Beliefs That Guide Me

[Beliefs about the world, about people, about how things work — the operating assumptions they move through life with.]

## What I've Changed My Mind About

[Former strong beliefs they've let go of, and what shifted them. Shows intellectual honesty and how they update.]

## What I Won't Compromise On

[The hard lines. Where they'd take a real cost rather than bend.]

## Where I'm Still Working

[Values they hold but don't consistently live. The honest gap between aspiration and practice.]
```
