# Aspirations and Fears

## What This File Is For

Where you're pointed and what you're afraid of — in life terms, not career terms. This is the last file in the portfolio, and it's here because an AI that knows what you're building toward and what scares you can orient everything it does around what actually matters to you. This file should be honest enough that reading it back feels slightly exposing. That's how you know it's real.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user articulate what they're hoping for and what they're afraid of — in the biggest sense. This is the final file, and by now you know this person well from the previous nine interviews. Use that context. Don't ask them to repeat what you already know — build on it. This file should feel like a natural conclusion to everything that came before. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. When you think about the next few years — not your career, your life — what are you hoping for? What are you building toward?
2. If things go really well, what's different about your life? Not accomplishments — what does it feel like?
3. What are you afraid of? Not abstract fears — the real ones that sit with you.
4. Is there something you want to be true about yourself that isn't yet? A version of yourself you're working toward?
5. What's the gap right now between where you are and where you want to be? How does that gap feel?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. End the portfolio on something real.

**After drafting:** Present the draft. Ask if it captures the actual weight of what they said — not a polished version, but the real thing. This is the file where being too clean is worse than being a little raw.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Aspirations and Fears

## What I'm Building Toward

[What they're hoping for in the biggest sense — not goals or milestones, but the life they're moving toward.]

## What "Going Well" Feels Like

[Not what success looks like from the outside — what it feels like from the inside. The subjective experience they're optimizing for.]

## What I'm Afraid Of

[Real fears. Not "I'm afraid of failure" — the specific versions. What keeps them up, what they avoid looking at, what they worry about when they're honest with themselves.]

## The Person I'm Becoming

[The version of themselves they're working toward. The aspirational identity — stated honestly, including the gap between here and there.]

## The Gap

[Where they are vs. where they want to be, and how that gap feels. Not a plan to close it — just an honest description of the distance.]
```
