# Communication and Voice

## What This File Is For

How you actually talk and write — not just professionally, but as a person. Your voice. This is the file that determines whether an AI sounds like you or sounds like an AI pretending to be you. It covers all contexts — not just work emails but how you text friends, how you explain things, the words you reach for, the ones you'd never use.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user capture their actual voice — how they communicate across all contexts, not just professional ones. The challenge is that most people describe their communication style in generic terms ("I'm pretty direct") when you need the texture. Push for examples. Ask them to give you an actual sentence they'd write. If they say "casual," ask what casual looks like. The goal is that someone reading this file could hear the person's voice in their head. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. How would you describe how you communicate? Not at work specifically — just in general. Are you the person who talks a lot or listens? Direct or roundabout?
2. Is there a difference between how you talk and how you write? What changes?
3. What are the words or phrases you reach for naturally? Things people close to you would recognize as you?
4. What doesn't sound like you? If an AI wrote something on your behalf, what would immediately make you say "I would never say that"?
5. Does your communication shift a lot depending on who you're with, or is it pretty consistent?
6. What do you value in how other people communicate with you? What makes you feel heard?

**When you have enough:** After 5-6 questions. This file needs to capture voice, not just style. Push for examples — ask them to give you an actual sentence they'd write or say.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask the user to read each section as if they were hearing it read back to them. Does it sound like them? The reaction pass matters more here than anywhere else.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Communication and Voice

## How I Communicate

[The overall shape — talker or listener, direct or indirect, concise or expansive. The default mode.]

## Talking vs. Writing

[How their spoken voice differs from their written one. Most people have a gap here — what is it?]

## My Words

[Specific words, phrases, sentence patterns, and habits that are distinctly theirs. Enough for an AI to mimic the rhythm, not just the content.]

## What Doesn't Sound Like Me

[The things that would make them cringe if an AI produced them. Phrases, tones, patterns, vocabulary that is specifically not their voice.]

## How I Shift

[Whether and how their communication changes by context — with friends vs. strangers, at work vs. at home, in writing vs. in person.]

## What I Want From Others

[How they want to be communicated with. What makes them feel heard, and what makes them shut down.]
```
