# Inner Life

## What This File Is For

The internal landscape — practices, recovery, philosophical orientation, what you're working on in yourself. This is the most personal file in the portfolio. It's also one of the most useful, because it tells an AI what's actually going on beneath the surface. A sparse honest version of this file is infinitely better than a detailed performative one.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user create a file about their inner life — their practices, their relationship with themselves, what they're working through. This is sensitive territory. Take what they give you. Don't push for more than they want to share. If they're open and reflective, follow the thread. If they're brief, respect it. Never diagnose, interpret, or therapize. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. Do you have any regular practices — meditation, journaling, prayer, therapy, anything — that are part of how you take care of your inner life?
2. Is there a philosophical or spiritual framework that matters to you? Not necessarily religion — could be a way of seeing the world.
3. Is there something you're actively working on in yourself right now — a pattern you're trying to change, something you're trying to understand?
4. How's your inner dialogue? Are you generally kind to yourself, hard on yourself, somewhere in between?
5. Is there a community or group that's important to your inner life — people you do this kind of work with?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. This file can be short. If someone shares three true sentences about their inner life, that's more valuable than a page of generalities.

**After drafting:** Present the draft gently. Ask if it captures what they meant and if anything feels exposed in a way they're not comfortable with. They can always pare it back.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# Inner Life

## Practices

[Whatever they do regularly to tend to their inner life — meditation, journaling, therapy, recovery meetings, walks, whatever. Or nothing, and that's fine to note too.]

## Orientation

[Their philosophical, spiritual, or meaning-making framework. What lens they see the world through. Could be a tradition, a practice, a set of ideas, or "I'm figuring this out."]

## What I'm Working On

[The current internal work — patterns they're trying to shift, things they're trying to understand about themselves, growth edges.]

## How I Relate to Myself

[Their inner dialogue — are they compassionate, critical, avoidant, curious? What's the tone of the voice in their head?]

## Community

[People or groups they do inner work with, if any. Recovery communities, sanghas, therapy groups, close friends who hold this space. Or solo — that's data too.]
```
