# How I Think

## What This File Is For

The shape of your mind — not what you know, but how you process, what grabs your attention, where you get stuck, and what comes naturally. This file helps an AI work with your thinking rather than against it. It's the difference between an assistant that explains things the way you already understand them and one that talks past you.

---

## Interview Protocol

*Hand this entire file to your AI interviewer and say "let's do this one." The interviewer should read the instructions below and run the interview.*

**Instructions for the interviewer:** You're helping the user understand and articulate their cognitive patterns — not their intelligence or credentials, but how their mind actually moves. Most people have never been asked to describe this explicitly, so they may need a moment. Give examples if they're stuck. Follow up when they describe something specific. Ask the following questions one at a time.

**Questions to ask:**

1. When you're trying to understand something new, what's your process? Do you read, tinker, talk it through, watch someone else do it?
2. What naturally grabs your attention? If you're scanning a room, a conversation, a problem — what do you notice first?
3. When you're stuck on something, what does "stuck" look like for you? What do you do?
4. Have you noticed patterns in how your mind works — things you're consistently good at or consistently miss?
5. Is there a type of thinking that feels effortless to you, and a type that feels like dragging a boulder uphill?

**When you have enough:** After 4-5 questions. This file should reveal how the person's mind actually moves — not what they're smart at.

**After drafting:** Present the draft and ask if the patterns described match their actual experience, or if they sound like flattering generalizations. Push for accuracy over compliment.

---

## Output Structure

```markdown
# How I Think

## How I Learn

[Their process for understanding new things — reading, doing, talking, observing, some combination. What works and what doesn't.]

## What Grabs My Attention

[Where their focus naturally goes — in conversations, problems, environments. What they notice that others miss, and what they miss that others notice.]

## How I Get Stuck (and Unstuck)

[What "stuck" looks like for them and what they do about it. Their patterns of avoidance, perseveration, or breakthrough.]

## Patterns I've Noticed

[Cognitive strengths and blind spots they're aware of. Where they're sharp, where they're reliably off.]

## What's Effortless vs. Effortful

[The types of thinking that come naturally and the types that drain them. The things they can do for hours and the things that exhaust them in minutes.]
```
